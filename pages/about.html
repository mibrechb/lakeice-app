About

This lake ice platform provides access to satellite-based observations of lake ice cover across the European Alps. It allows users to explore changes in the timing and duration of seasonal lake ice over the past decade and to compare patterns across more than a thousand lakes. The platform was developed as an open and transparent way to share results from the lake ice monitoring framework presented in Brechbühler et al. (in preparation).

Why monitor lake ice?

Seasonal lake ice plays an important ecological, climatic, and cultural role. Changes in ice cover can affect lake ecosystems and water quality, for example by altering mixing, oxygen conditions, and the timing of biological processes.

As the climate warms, the seasonal timing of lake freeze-up and break-up — commonly referred to as lake ice phenology — is changing. Across many lakes in the Northern Hemisphere, ice tends to form later, disappear earlier, and persist for shorter periods, while some lakes increasingly experience winters without complete ice cover.

Because lake ice responds sensitively to changes in climate, it is also an important indicator of environmental change. Lake ice cover is recognised by the Global Climate Observing System (GCOS) as part of the Essential Climate Variables, highlighting the importance of consistent observations for understanding and monitoring the Earth's climate system.

How does it work?

The monitoring framework combines optical, thermal, and active microwave satellite observations to detect lake ice from space. By integrating information from multiple satellite sensors with spatial resolutions of approximately 30 metres, the system can provide observations from sub-weekly to, under favourable conditions, near-daily intervals.

A complete processing workflow is used to derive lake ice cover and seasonal phenology for lakes as small as 0.05 km². Machine-learning classification models are applied to the satellite observations to distinguish different surface conditions and reconstruct the seasonal evolution of lake ice.
